learning is the process of building mental models. not memorizing facts, but constructing {fold: schemas | organized frameworks of knowledge that help us understand new information faster. schemas are the reason experts learn faster than beginners — they have more hooks to hang new ideas on.}

## core principles

- **active recall** beats passive reading by a large margin
- **interleaving** topics during practice improves long-term transfer
- **elaborative interrogation** — ask *why*, not just *what*
- **desirable difficulty** — struggle during learning is a signal it's working
- **spaced repetition** (see [[memory]] for the mechanism)

::: card
the best learning happens at the edge of your current understanding — not too easy, not too hard. this zone of productive struggle is called **desirable difficulty**.
:::

## the feynman technique

::: branch feynman technique — step by step
1. choose a concept you want to understand
2. explain it as if teaching a child — no jargon
3. identify the gaps in your explanation (these are what you don't truly understand)
4. go back to source material and fill those gaps
5. simplify further and use analogies

this works because teaching reveals what you don't actually understand. the gaps become visible.
:::

## what doesn't work

- **re-reading** — feels productive but is largely ineffective
- **highlighting** — low-effort, minimal retention benefit
- **massed practice** (cramming) — fast to acquire, fast to forget

## connection to memory

you cannot separate learning from [[memory]]. encoding, consolidation, and retrieval are all part of the same cycle. improving one improves the other.
